Context

teachers.tn is a Tunisian education platform. Teachers can browse free materials, buy structured resource packs, read blog content, and preview sample documents and videos before downloading. The site is Arabic-first, which is not a translation layer: the layout, typography, navigation, and content hierarchy all have to work in RTL.

The product is live and has been operated continuously. It is not an AI case study; it is here because it shows the product and delivery side of my work: content modelling, commerce, SEO, performance, and the maintenance that follows launch.

My role

I built and operate the platform end to end: the Next.js application, content structure, Supabase storage integration, deployment, Arabic content presentation, and the public-facing SEO surface. The work includes the storefront and the editorial layer around it.

Scope note

This is my own product work, not a client project presented as a team build. The PDFs and videos shown on the site are the platform’s actual resources; customer and purchase data are not used in this case study.

Architecture

The site uses Next.js with the App Router and React Server Components, Tailwind for styling, Supabase object storage for resource files, and Netlify for deployment. Server rendering matters here: course pages, resource previews, and editorial pages need to be indexable and fast on mobile connections.

  • Server-rendered catalogue. Course and resource detail pages are generated from structured content, not hand-built one at a time.
  • Object storage. Sample documents and media assets are served from Supabase rather than bundled into the application.
  • Arabic RTL. Direction-aware layout, Arabic typography, and content hierarchy are part of the component design.
  • Static delivery. Netlify handles the public deployment and preview workflow.

Content and commerce

The catalogue mixes free materials with paid packs priced between 25 and 80 TND. The payment flow is deliberately simple for the Tunisian market: users choose a pack, pay by bank transfer, and submit proof for manual verification. The product is designed around that constraint rather than pretending a card-first checkout is universal.

Around the catalogue, editorial pages answer teacher questions and connect search intent to the relevant resources. That content is part of the acquisition strategy, not decoration.

SEO and delivery

The public sitemap contains 49 routes across courses, resources, and editorial pages. I built the metadata and content structure so those routes describe real pages rather than empty templates. The site is served as a static deployment on Netlify with Supabase handling stored files.

I do not publish traffic or revenue numbers. The evidence here is the live site, its route structure, and the production screens.

Limits

  • No public performance data. I do not quote search rankings, conversion, or revenue.
  • Manual commerce. Bank-transfer verification is a market constraint, not a claim of a fully automated checkout.
  • Ongoing maintenance. A content platform is never “finished”; route coverage and content quality are continuous work.

What I would improve

  • Add structured data for course and resource pages to improve search presentation.
  • Build a clearer progress and saved-resources experience for returning users.
  • Measure Core Web Vitals on mid-range mobile devices and optimise the heaviest previews.
  • Automate more of the purchase verification workflow without weakening fraud checks.
