Context

D30 Challenge is a 30-day fitness product built around a simple idea: give users a structured plan instead of an empty workout tracker. The app combines a course library, a weekly workout plan, nutrition guidance, and progress views.

The product is live in both stores. That matters for this page because it is not a prototype or a concept render: the screens below come from the shipped app.

My contribution

I contributed to the mobile product during a 2024 internship at Smartegy. The app was a team effort. I do not claim sole ownership of the backend, the release process, or the product design, and the private source remains with the company.

The public evidence is the shipped build and its store listings. That is enough to discuss the product surface and the engineering stack; it is not enough to assign every commit to one person.

Scope note

This page describes work on a team-built product. I keep the language at the level the public evidence supports: shipped mobile implementation, cross-platform UI, and integration with a REST backend.

The product

  • Course discovery. Structured fitness programmes with sessions that fit a 30-day plan.
  • Weekly plan. A day-by-day view of workouts, rest, and progression.
  • Nutrition guidance. Meal and habit content that supports the training plan.
  • Progress. Tracking views that make the challenge feel cumulative rather than episodic.
  • Live sessions. Zoom-integrated workout scheduling, per the public product description and resume.

Implementation

The mobile frontend is Flutter, which let the same product surface ship to iOS and Android. The app talks to a Laravel REST backend for account, content, and plan data. The work sits at the product boundary: mobile screens, interaction states, and integration with the APIs the backend exposes.

Shipping to two stores also means handling the unglamorous parts: store metadata, screenshots, build variants, and the small layout differences that appear on real devices. Those are part of the product, even when they never show up in a feature list.

Limits

  • Team ownership. The product was built by a team; individual commit history is not public.
  • No usage metrics. I do not have public retention, revenue, or engagement numbers, so I do not quote any.
  • Privacy. I deliberately do not use the progress-record screen from the store listing because it exposes a demo user’s weight data.
  • Stack boundaries. Flutter and Laravel describe the product stack; they do not imply that every layer was written by me.

What I would improve

  • Add accessibility labels and dynamic-type checks across the plan and nutrition screens.
  • Design explicit offline and poor-network states, not just the happy path.
  • Make progress data easier to understand and easier to delete.
  • Add automated screenshot and layout checks before store submissions.
